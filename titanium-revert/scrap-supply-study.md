# Titanium Scrap Supply Study

2026-10-16 · @Hunter

## Bottom line

A new buyer can realistically get about **500 t of titanium scrap in year 1, 1,250 t in year 2 and 2,450 t in year 3**, counting from the first purchase. The low case is 150, 435 and 700 t. The high case is 1,375, 3,700 and 5,350 t.

- **Plant fit.** A 3,500 t a year plant needs about 3,800 t of feed. The base case covers 14% of that in year 1 and 65% in year 3.

- **Your 1,200 t guess.** Year 2 of the base case is 1,260 t, so the guess holds for year 2. Outbidding alone reaches about 900 t by year 3 (1,500 t in the high case). Every ton above that comes from a cleaning line for off-grade turnings, contracts won at renewal, one program with a big manufacturer, and imports.

- **Locked supply.** About 70% of US-made titanium scrap never reaches an open buyer, and US melters already import about 32,000 t a year. The clean, uncommitted US pool is 7,000–10,000 t. TIMET's and Perryman's new furnaces alone need 9,000–12,000 t more.

- **Plant size.** This supply supports a plant that blends scrap with sponge, or a first hearth of about 2,000 t a year. At the plan's prices, the base-case blend earns about $34M of gross margin a year, against $41M on all scrap.

- **Timing.** A supplier book takes about three years to build. To have year-3 tonnage at first melt, buying starts around 2028.

Every tonnage here is an estimate. USGS has withheld US scrap use since 2021, and page fetching was blocked in this environment, so figures come from web-search summaries of the linked pages. The 60-day plan at the end replaces the estimates with named tons.

## Where US titanium scrap goes

US titanium melters use about 55,000 t of scrap a year, and more than half of it is imported. Of the roughly 46,000 t made in the US, only half goes back to US melters, mostly as their own in-house scrap.

*[Figure: Rebuilt from USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2021–2026, USGS Minerals Yearbooks and Argus trade data · 2025 estimates. The live doc shows the drawing.]*

| Flow, 2025 estimate | Tons a year |
| --- | --- |
| Imported scrap to US titanium melters | 32,000 |
| US-made scrap to US titanium melters | 23,000 |
| US-made scrap exported | 12,000 |
| US-made scrap to steel, as ferrotitanium | 9,000 |
| US-made scrap to superalloys and other | 2,000 |

- **The blue pool.** Exports and steel take the one large pool a new US melter would add to domestic supply. Most of it is mixed, oily or oxidized turnings that need cleaning, sorting and dilution before a hearth can melt them.

- **Export buyers.** India, Canada, the UK and Mexico were the top destinations in late 2024, when exports hit a four-year high ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2656341-us-ti-scrap-imports-exports-fell-in-4q)).

- **Data gap.** USGS last published scrap use by industry for 2020 ([MCS 2021](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2021/mcs2021-titanium.pdf)) and has withheld it since ([MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf)). These flows are rebuilt from sponge imports, scrap trade and scrap's historical 54–60% share of melt input. Two separate rebuilds differ by about 10,000 t on melter use, and both put the clean, uncommitted US pool under 10,000 t.

About 70% of US-made titanium scrap never reaches an open buyer (range 55–80%). It stays inside the melter that made it, inside shops the melters own, or goes back to a named mill under contract.

- **What's left.** About 15,000 t a year (9,000–20,000 t) from independent aerospace shops, defense, space, medical and industrial work. Only 7,000–10,000 t of it is clean enough to melt without heavy cleaning; the rest goes to ferrotitanium or export.

- **Demand coming for it.** TIMET's $500M [Ravenswood, West Virginia plant](https://www.wsaz.com/2024/06/12/timet-titanium-facility-under-construction-offers-200-local-positions) (one electron beam hearth, seven VAR furnaces) and [Perryman's growth to 42M lb](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/perryman-boosting-melt-capacity) need roughly 9,000–12,000 t more scrap a year. ATI doubled its [Richland hearth](https://www.atimaterials.com/business/Documents/2025%20Fact%20Sheet_ATI%20Richland_2025.04.17.pdf) in 2025. IperionX needs about 1,400 t a year by mid-2027, and 6K Additive targets 1,000 t.

- **Scrap-use capacity.** ELG's titanium head told the industry that US capacity to use scrap rises about 22% in 2026, to about 111,000 t ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103593647/US-Titanium-Sector-to-Hike-Scrap-Use-by-22-in-2026-Amid-Market-Shifts)).

- **Today's slack.** US scrap was oversupplied in 2024–25, and the supply chain held about 18 months of titanium inventory in October 2025 ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2737772-ti-demand-rebound-dependent-on-inventory-draws)). That slack shrinks as the new furnaces qualify through 2026–28.

- **Leaks.** The Howmet suit shows a high enough price pulls scrap out of a locked loop. That is how a newcomer reaches locked scrap, and it works mainly in a tight market.

The capture model counts implant and new-factory scrap inside the first row.

- **Boneyard.** About 4,000 aircraft and 6,650 engines sit in storage at Davis-Monthan, but the Air Force harvests parts first and the airframes are mostly aluminum.

- **Sorting losses.** In a 2025 DLA test at Fort Riley, more than 1 t of titanium-bearing scrap gave about 340 lb of usable feed ([DLA](https://www.dla.mil/About-DLA/News/News-Article-View/Article/4301284/titanium-recovery-furthers-critical-materials-reuse-effort/)). Government lots shrink once sorted.

- **Consumer electronics.** Close to zero. Titanium iPhone frames were machined in Asia, and the iPhone 17 Pro went back to aluminum in September 2025.

- **3D-printing waste.** About 100–300 t a year, mostly claimed by powder makers such as 6K Additive and Continuum Powders.

## What it takes to win scrap

A 3–8% premium over the local bid, paid within about two weeks, moves small shops. Large generators move only when their contracts end, so each big win takes 1–3 years.

| Grade (US, delivered to a dealer or processor)    | $/lb     | As of        |
|---------------------------------------------------|-----------|--------------|
| 6-4 aerospace turnings                            | 2.45–2.75 | Jan 2026     |
| 6-4 bulk weldable solids                          | 2.50–2.90 | Jan 2026     |
| Commercially pure grade 1 solids                  | 2.80–3.15 | Jan 2026     |
| 85% titanium mixed turnings (ferrotitanium grade) | 0.28–0.35 | Dec 2025     |
| Imported scrap, average ($7.70/kg)               | 3.49      | Jan–Jul 2025 |

Prices from [Argus sample reports](https://www.argusmedia.com/-/media/project/argusmedia/mainsite/english/documents-and-files/sample-reports/argus-scrap-markets.pdf?rev=033d6faa07574583ba8451f4e5456800) (the January 2026 year is inferred from the page) and [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf). Bulk weldable is about half its 2023 peak.

- **Premium.** No buyer publishes one. At $2.60/lb, a 5% premium is $0.13/lb, or about $0.6M a year on 2,000 t. The ceiling is the delivered index price minus your own cleaning cost, since a melter buying direct also keeps the processor's margin.

- **Terms.** Free bins, scheduled pickup, payment in 7–15 days, and a published formula priced as a percent of the Argus index with a floor. Aerospace contracts already use Argus prices for their surcharges.

- **Certified lots.** Grade 9 mis-sorted as grade 5 gets a whole load rejected. Sellers want an assay for each lot, and buyers want custody records.

- **Every grade.** Incumbents take solids, turnings and commercially pure grades. A buyer that wants only 6-4 solids loses the shop.

- **Mill approval.** Mega Metals is one of the few US processors titanium mills approve to handle reusable 6-4 turnings. Buying turnings direct means crushing, degreasing, drying, magnetic separation, alloy sorting and inclusion control.

- **Entry path.** The research found no newcomer that built up US titanium scrap buying from scratch in the last decade. Thalheimer bought Mega Metals in 2018, Venture Metals bought both in 2025, and Kymera bought AmeriTi in 2022. IperionX, 6K Additive and EcoTitanium got scrap through government or a customer instead.

## Capture model

In the base case, outbidding for clean scrap gives about 900 t a year by year 3. The other 1,550 t needs a cleaning line, a contract won at renewal, depot and teardown deals, and imports.

| Pool                         | Base share of the pool by year 3 | What moves it                                                              |
|------------------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Outbidding for clean scrap   | About 10%                        | Premium size, service, and how hard Ravenswood and Perryman bid            |
| Cleaning off-grade turnings  | About 3%                         | Whether a cleaning line runs by year 2, owned or tolled                    |
| Military depots and DLA      | About 30%                        | A Pentagon award that brings depot scrap, as IperionX and 6K Additive have |
| Retired aircraft and engines | About 20%                        | Teardown deals with AerSale, AAR or engine lessors                         |
| One big-generator contract   | About 2% of the locked loops     | Winning one defense prime, Tier-1 or OEM loop at renewal                   |
| Imported scrap               | About 1%                         | Price against US melters, and Eurasian buyers pulling European scrap east  |

- **Low case.** No cleaning line, no contracts, and an aerospace recovery that has incumbents bidding for every ton.

- **Base case.** A 3–8% premium, bins and fast payment, a cleaning line by year 2, one mid-size contract by year 3, and some imports.

- **High case.** Buying a small processor in year 1 with its supplier book, one OEM or Tier-1 program, and active importing.

- **Cross-check.** Three research passes reached this range separately. One put outbidding plus contracts at about 500 t in year 1 and 2,000 t in year 3. Another put a newcomer at 700–2,500 t a year. A third found about 700 t at start from non-traditional pools.

## Why nobody has built this

Every US titanium melter already melts scrap; at TIMET it is 30–50% of input. What hasn't happened in decades is a new, independent US melter starting from scratch. Seven reasons explain it, and the first four bear directly on this study.

1.  **Scrap in use.** Every clean ton a newcomer wins comes out of an incumbent's furnace. Only the scrap now exported or sent to steel is new to US supply, and it is the dirtiest.

2.  **Qualification.** Buyers pay for a track record. [EcoTitanium](https://www.usinenouvelle.com/article/eramet-inaugure-ecotitanium-une-usine-de-fabrication-de-titane-par-recyclage.N587628) opened in 2017 still qualifying common grades, with a five-year ramp planned. The plan's $12/lb slab price assumes qualified metal.

3.  **Slab buyers.** US titanium is vertically integrated, and TIMET, ATI, Howmet and Perryman melt for their own mills. A melter without a plate mill sells slab to a competitor.

4.  **Price swings.** 6-4 turnings sold for 5–35¢/lb in July 2009 and $2.95–3.05 fourteen months later ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2151068-ti-scrap-supply-may-face-bullwhip-shortage)). European turnings fell to $0.60–0.65 in mid-2020 ([Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/scrap-shortage-from-aviation-pushes-european-titanium-prices-to-multi-year-highs/)). Scrap and slab move together, so the margin is the gap between them, and a one-plant company with heavy debt can run out of cash in one downturn.

5.  **Working capital.** A plant selling $92M a year carries roughly $20–30M of metal and unpaid invoices (estimate: 3–4 months of inventory plus 45–60 days of receivables). The plan's $55–75M capex leaves it out.

6.  **Customer-owned precedents.** Aubert & Duval, which runs EcoTitanium, is controlled by Airbus and Safran, the firms that supply its scrap and buy its ingot; it plans to pass 10,000 t by 2029 ([Usine Nouvelle](https://www.usinenouvelle.com/matieres-premieres/mines-metaux/metaux-critiques-et-strategiques/un-projet-de-souverainete-aubert-duval-prevoit-daugmenter-ses-capacites-de-production-et-de-recyclage-de-titane-pour-laeronautique-en-france.NAWY2Q2BTRC4NEINGEJZOF3D3I.html)). Titanium Hearth Technologies, a 1990s US hearth melter, was a [TIMET joint venture](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001011657/000095012996003270/0000950129-96-003270.txt) and ended up inside TIMET. [Perryman](https://www.observer-reporter.com/business/2024/sep/07/perryman-co-expanding-its-horizons/), founded in 1988 by a 35-year industry veteran, added melting only in 2007.

7.  **New capacity, people and fire.** Ravenswood, Perryman, ATI Richland, American Titanium Metal's $868M North Carolina campus and Aubert & Duval's expansion all land around 2030. Experienced melt-shop people work at about four US companies. Titanium chips burn and fine powder explodes; a 2010 dust explosion at AL Solutions in West Virginia killed three workers.

## What this means for the plan

The base case supports a plant that blends scrap with sponge, not a 3,500 t all-scrap plant in its first three years. At the plan's prices, each 1,000 t of sponge in place of scrap costs about $5M of gross margin a year.

| Year-3 case, 3,800 t of feed | Scrap   | Sponge  | Gross margin a year |
|------------------------------|---------|---------|---------------------|
| Plan, all scrap              | 3,800 t | 0       | $41M               |
| Base                         | 2,450 t | 1,350 t | $34M               |
| Base, US scrap only          | 2,050 t | 1,750 t | $32M               |
| Low                          | 700 t   | 3,100 t | $26M               |

Assumes the plan's $12/lb slab, $3.10/lb scrap delivered and cleaned, and sponge about $2.30/lb dearer than scrap.

1.  **Slab price outweighs scrap.** Each $1/lb of slab price moves gross margin by $7.7M, more than the gap between the base case and all-scrap. Qualification and a named slab buyer matter more than the last 1,000 t of scrap.

2.  **Plant size.** Three fits for base-case supply. Build the 3,500 t plant and blend about 35% sponge by year 3. Or build a first hearth of about 2,000 t a year and add a second when supply proves out. Or build for 3,500 t and run it part-loaded at first, at a higher cost per pound.

3.  **Buying starts before the furnace.** The plan builds from 2030 and reaches full output around 2033. Year 3 of buying should line up with first melt, so buying starts about 2028. The three ways in are buying a small processor with its supplier book, a toll-cleaning deal with a processor such as Mega Metals or a CRONIMET unit, or a Pentagon award that brings depot scrap.

4.  **National security story.** Only scrap that now leaves the country or goes into steel adds American titanium. About a quarter of the base case is that kind (cleaning-line, depot and aircraft tons, about 650 t in year 3). DIBC and Office of Strategic Capital reviewers will ask how many tons are new to US supply, and the cleaning line is the answer.

5.  **Sponge risk.** A blend puts the plant in line for the same imported sponge as the incumbents, mostly from Japan. Price sponge supply in the site study, not after it.

6.  **Gate B.** The plan's Gate B needs 2,000+ t a year signed. The base case reaches that in year 3 of buying; the low case never does.

7.  **Go or no-go.** Go if the 60-day calls below find at least 1,000 t a year of named, uncommitted scrap with sellers willing to sign letters of intent. Below 500 t, the scrap-led melt shop needs a customer-owned loop, as EcoTitanium has, or a smaller plan.

## How to prove the number in 60 days

Three data pulls and 30 generator calls turn this estimate into named tons. The test is 1,000 t a year of named, uncommitted scrap by day 60.

### Weeks 1–2: data pulls

- [ ] Register for USA Trade Online (free) and pull titanium scrap (HTS 8108.30) imports and exports by country and quantity, 2019–2026.
- [ ] Ask the USGS titanium commodity specialist for the withheld scrap figures in aggregate, and price International Titanium Association membership for its statistics.
- [ ] Ask Argus for a trial of its titanium scrap prices back to 2009.

### Weeks 2–6: calls

- [ ] 15 independent aerospace machine shops (Tier 2–3) outside the Boeing and F-35 loops
- [ ] 5 defense and space factories, such as Anduril, Hadrian, Castelion, and SpaceX or Blue Origin suppliers
- [ ] 5 implant makers in Warsaw, Indiana and Memphis
- [ ] 5 government sources, such as DLA Disposition Services, the Tinker, Ogden and Robins air logistics complexes, and a Navy Fleet Readiness Center
- [ ] 4 processors and dealers (Mega Metals, CRONIMET's United Alloys & Metals, Greystone, Goldman), for toll-cleaning prices and whether an owner would sell
- [ ] 2 equipment vendors, for a budgetary quote on a 1,000–2,000 t a year turnings cleaning line

Ask every generator the same five questions, and log the answers in one sheet (supplier, grade, pounds a month, current buyer, contract end date, letter-of-intent status).

1.  How many pounds a month, by grade (6-4 solids, 6-4 turnings, commercially pure)?

2.  Who buys it now, on what price formula, and when does that deal end?

3.  Does a customer contract, such as Boeing's D204, control where it goes?

4.  What would make you switch buyers?

5.  Would you sign a non-binding letter of intent?

### Weeks 6–8: decide

| Named, uncommitted tons a year by day 60 | Reading              | Next step                                                                     |
|------------------------------------------|----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1,000+ with 3+ letters of intent         | Base case holds      | Keep the 3,500 t plan with a sponge blend, and plan the buying desk for 2028  |
| 500–1,000                                | Between low and base | Plan a 2,000 t first hearth, and price a processor purchase or a toll partner |
| Under 500                                | Low case             | Pursue a customer-owned or partner-owned plant before spending on furnaces    |
