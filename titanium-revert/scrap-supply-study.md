# Titanium Scrap Supply Study

2026-10-16 · @Hunter

## Bottom line

The base case is about **525 t of titanium scrap in buying year 1, 1,260 t in year 2 and 2,455 t in year 3**. The low case is 150, 435 and 695 t. The high case is 1,375, 3,690 and 5,350 t.

- **Plant fit.** A 3,500 t a year plant needs about 3,800 t of feed. The base case reaches 14% of that in buying year 1 and 65% in year 3.

- **Your 1,200 t guess.** Year 2 of the base case is 1,260 t, so the guess holds for year 2. Outbidding alone reaches about 900 t by year 3 (1,500 t in the high case). The rest comes from a cleaning line, one big-generator contract won at renewal, depot and teardown deals, and imports.

- **Open supply.** Roughly half of US-made titanium scrap never reaches an open buyer, and US melters already import about 32,000 t a year. The clean, uncommitted US pool is estimated at 7,000–10,000 t. TIMET's and Perryman's new furnaces alone need an estimated 9,000–12,000 t more.

- **Plant size.** This supply supports a plant that blends scrap with sponge, or a first hearth of about 2,000 t a year. At the plan's prices, the base-case blend earns about $34M of gross margin a year, against $41M on all scrap.

- **Timing.** Gate B asks for 2,000+ t a year signed before the plant is financed, and a supplier book takes about three years. Buying starts around 2027–28, through a desk that resells to melters until first melt in about 2032.

Every tonnage here is an estimate. USGS has withheld US scrap use since 2021. Source pages were not opened directly, so figures come from web-search summaries of the linked pages. The 60-day plan at the end tests the estimates against named tons.

## Where US titanium scrap goes

US titanium melters use about 55,000 t of scrap a year, and more than half of it is imported. Of the roughly 46,000 t made in the US, half goes back to US melters, mostly through locked loops. Nearly half leaves the titanium supply chain.

*[Figure: Rebuilt from USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2021–2026, USGS Minerals Yearbooks and Argus trade data · 2025 estimates. The live doc shows the drawing.]*

| Flow, 2025 estimate | Tons a year |
| --- | --- |
| Imported scrap to US titanium melters | 32,000 |
| US-made scrap to US melters through locked loops | 20,000 |
| US-made scrap to US melters on the open market | 3,000 |
| US-made scrap exported | 12,000 |
| US-made scrap to steel, as ferrotitanium | 9,000 |
| US-made scrap to superalloys and other | 2,000 |

- **Exports and steel.** This is the one large pool a new US melter would add to domestic supply. Most of it is mixed, oily or oxidized turnings that need cleaning, sorting and dilution before a hearth can melt them, though some clean scrap is exported too.

- **Export buyers.** India, Canada, the UK and Mexico were the top destinations in late 2024. Full-year 2024 exports were a four-year high, though they fell in the fourth quarter ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2656341-us-ti-scrap-imports-exports-fell-in-4q)).

- **Data gap.** USGS last published scrap use by industry for 2020 ([MCS 2021](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2021/mcs2021-titanium.pdf)) and has withheld it since ([MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf)). These flows are rebuilt from sponge imports, scrap trade and scrap's historical 54–60% share of melt input. A second rebuild put melter use about 7,000 t lower and the locked share near 70%. Both put the clean, uncommitted US pool under 10,000 t.

## Who already has it

Roughly half of US-made titanium scrap never reaches an open buyer. Depending on how the withheld USGS data are rebuilt, the locked share runs from about 40% to 70%, and the flow above uses about 45%. Locked scrap stays inside the melter that made it or shops the melters own, or goes back to a named mill under contract.

- **What's left.** About 26,000 t a year reaches the open market. An estimated 7,000–10,000 t of it is clean enough to melt without heavy cleaning. Another 12,000–18,000 t is off-grade and goes to ferrotitanium or export, and about 2,000 t goes to superalloys.

- **Demand coming for it.** TIMET is building a $500M plant at [Ravenswood, West Virginia](https://www.wsaz.com/2024/06/12/timet-titanium-facility-under-construction-offers-200-local-positions), with one electron beam hearth and seven VAR furnaces. [Perryman is growing to 42M lb](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/perryman-boosting-melt-capacity), about 19,000 t, of melt a year. Together they need an estimated 9,000–12,000 t more scrap a year. IperionX needs about 1,400 t a year by mid-2027, and 6K Additive targets 1,000 t.

- **Start dates.** ATI's doubled [Richland hearth](https://www.atimaterials.com/business/Documents/2025%20Fact%20Sheet_ATI%20Richland_2025.04.17.pdf) started in 2025, and Ravenswood was still under construction in August 2025. New furnaces take years to qualify, so their scrap demand should build through 2026–28.

- **Scrap use.** ELG's titanium head told the industry that US scrap use rises about 22% in 2026 as new furnaces start ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103593647/US-Titanium-Sector-to-Hike-Scrap-Use-by-22-in-2026-Amid-Market-Shifts)).

- **Today's slack.** US scrap was oversupplied in 2024–25, and the supply chain held about 18 months of titanium inventory in October 2025 ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2737772-ti-demand-rebound-dependent-on-inventory-draws)). That slack should shrink as the new furnaces qualify.

- **Leaks.** The Howmet suit shows a high enough price pulls scrap out of a locked loop. That is how a newcomer reaches locked scrap, and it works mainly in a tight market.

The capture model counts implant and new-factory scrap inside the first row.

- **Boneyard.** About 4,000 aircraft and 6,650 engines sit in storage at Davis-Monthan, but the Air Force harvests parts first and the airframes are mostly aluminum.

- **Sorting losses.** In a 2025 DLA test at Fort Riley, more than 1 t of titanium-bearing scrap gave about 150 kg (340 lb) of usable feed, roughly 15% ([DLA](https://www.dla.mil/About-DLA/News/News-Article-View/Article/4301284/titanium-recovery-furthers-critical-materials-reuse-effort/)). Government lots shrink once sorted.

- **Consumer electronics.** Close to zero. Titanium iPhone frames were machined in Asia, and the iPhone 17 Pro went back to aluminum in September 2025.

- **3D-printing waste.** About 100–300 t a year, mostly claimed by powder makers such as 6K Additive and Continuum Powders.

## What it takes to win scrap

An estimated 3–8% premium over the local bid, paid within about two weeks, moves small shops. Large generators move only when their contracts end, so each big win takes 1–3 years.

| Grade (US, delivered to a dealer or processor)    | $/lb     | As of        |
|---------------------------------------------------|-----------|--------------|
| 6-4 aerospace turnings                            | 2.45–2.75 | Jan 2026     |
| 6-4 bulk weldable solids                          | 2.50–2.90 | Jan 2026     |
| Commercially pure grade 1 solids                  | 2.80–3.15 | Jan 2026     |
| 85% titanium mixed turnings (ferrotitanium grade) | 0.28–0.35 | Dec 2025     |
| Imported scrap, average ($7.70/kg)               | 3.49      | Jan–Jul 2025 |

Prices from [Argus sample reports](https://www.argusmedia.com/-/media/project/argusmedia/mainsite/english/documents-and-files/sample-reports/argus-scrap-markets.pdf?rev=033d6faa07574583ba8451f4e5456800) (the January 2026 year is inferred from the page) and [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf). Bulk weldable is about half its 2023 peak.

- **Premium.** No buyer publishes one. At $2.60/lb, a 5% premium is $0.13/lb, about $0.26M a year on the 900 t outbid in base year 3. That premium assumes today's slack. For a plant that blends sponge, the ceiling is the scrap price that costs as much as the sponge it replaces, about $2/lb above today's index. Incumbents face the same ceiling, so a bidding war is the real risk, and it is the low case.

- **Terms.** Free bins, scheduled pickup, payment in 7–15 days, and a published formula priced as a percent of the Argus index with a floor. Aerospace contracts already use Argus prices for their surcharges.

- **Certified lots.** Grade 9 mis-sorted as grade 5 gets a whole load rejected. Sellers want an assay for each lot, and buyers want custody records.

- **Every grade.** Incumbents take solids, turnings and commercially pure grades. A buyer that wants only 6-4 solids loses the shop.

- **Mill approval.** Mega Metals is one of the few US processors titanium mills approve to handle reusable 6-4 turnings. Buying turnings direct means crushing, degreasing, drying, magnetic separation, alloy sorting and inclusion control.

- **Entry path.** The research found no newcomer that built up US titanium scrap buying from scratch in the last decade. Thalheimer bought Mega Metals in 2018, Kymera bought AmeriTi in 2022, and Venture Metals bought Thalheimer and Mega Metals in 2025. IperionX, 6K Additive and EcoTitanium got scrap through government or a customer instead.

## Capture model

In the base case, outbidding for clean scrap gives about 900 t a year by year 3. The other 1,550 t needs a cleaning line, a contract won at renewal, depot and teardown deals, and imports.

|------------------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Outbidding for clean scrap   | About 10%                        | Premium size, service, and how hard Ravenswood and Perryman bid            |
| Cleaning off-grade turnings  | About 3%                         | Tolled cleaning in year 1, then an owned or dedicated line by year 2       |
| Military depots and DLA      | About 30%                        | A Pentagon award that brings depot scrap, as IperionX and 6K Additive have |
| Retired aircraft and engines | About 20%                        | Teardown deals with AerSale, AAR or engine lessors                         |
| One big-generator contract   | About 2% of the locked loops     | Winning one defense prime, Tier-1 or OEM loop at renewal                   |
| Imported scrap               | About 1%                         | Price against US melters, and Eurasian buyers pulling European scrap east  |

- **Low case.** Tolled cleaning at small volume, no contracts, and an aerospace recovery that has incumbents bidding for every ton.

- **Base case.** A 3–8% premium, bins and fast payment, tolled cleaning in year 1 and an owned or dedicated line by year 2, one big-generator contract by year 3, and some imports.

- **High case.** Buying a small processor in year 1 with its supplier book, one OEM or Tier-1 program, and active importing.

- **Tons basis.** Tons are as bought. Cleaning losses cut melt-ready tons, most of all for off-grade turnings. The base case's 500 t of off-grade feed in year 3 is about 13% of a 3,800 t charge, and the trial melts should set how much a hearth can dilute.

- **Cross-check.** Three of the four research passes, drawing on overlapping sources, gave similar figures. One put outbidding plus contracts at about 500 t in year 1 and 2,000 t in year 3. Another put a newcomer at 700–2,500 t a year. A third found about 700 t at start from non-traditional pools, above the base year 1.

## Why nobody has built this

Every US titanium melter already melts scrap, and scrap is more than half of US melt input. No new, independent US melter has started since Perryman added melting in 2007. Seven reasons explain it, and the first four bear directly on this study.

1.  **Scrap in use.** Almost every clean ton a newcomer wins comes out of an incumbent's furnace. Only scrap now exported or sent to steel is new to US supply, and most of it is the dirtiest.

2.  **Qualification.** Buyers pay for a track record. [EcoTitanium](https://www.usinenouvelle.com/article/eramet-inaugure-ecotitanium-une-usine-de-fabrication-de-titane-par-recyclage.N587628) opened in 2017 and was still qualifying common grades then, with a five-year ramp planned. The plan's $12/lb slab price assumes qualified metal.

3.  **Slab buyers.** US titanium is vertically integrated, and TIMET, ATI, Howmet and Perryman melt for their own mills. A melter without a plate mill sells slab to a competitor.

4.  **Price swings.** 6-4 turnings sold for 5–35¢/lb in July 2009 and $2.95–3.05 fourteen months later ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2151068-ti-scrap-supply-may-face-bullwhip-shortage)). European turnings fell to $0.60–0.65 in mid-2020 ([Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/scrap-shortage-from-aviation-pushes-european-titanium-prices-to-multi-year-highs/)). Slab prices lag scrap by months, so the gap between them swings. In a downturn, volume and price fall together, and a one-plant company with heavy debt can run out of cash.

5.  **Working capital.** A plant selling $92M a year carries roughly $20–30M of metal and unpaid invoices (estimate: 3–4 months of inventory plus 45–60 days of receivables). The plan's $55–75M capex leaves it out.

6.  **Ownership precedents.** Aubert & Duval, which runs EcoTitanium, is controlled by Airbus and Safran, the firms that supply its scrap and buy its ingot. It plans to pass 10,000 t of titanium ingot a year by 2029 ([Usine Nouvelle](https://www.usinenouvelle.com/matieres-premieres/mines-metaux/metaux-critiques-et-strategiques/un-projet-de-souverainete-aubert-duval-prevoit-daugmenter-ses-capacites-de-production-et-de-recyclage-de-titane-pour-laeronautique-en-france.NAWY2Q2BTRC4NEINGEJZOF3D3I.html)). Titanium Hearth Technologies, a 1990s US hearth melter, was a [TIMET joint venture](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001011657/000095012996003270/0000950129-96-003270.txt) and ended up inside TIMET. [Perryman](https://www.observer-reporter.com/business/2024/sep/07/perryman-co-expanding-its-horizons/), founded in 1988 by a 35-year industry veteran, added melting only in 2007.

7.  **New capacity, people and fire.** Ravenswood, Perryman, ATI Richland, American Titanium Metal's $868M North Carolina campus and Aubert & Duval's expansion add capacity between 2025 and 2029. Experienced melt-shop people work at about four US companies. Titanium chips burn and fine powder explodes; a 2010 dust explosion at AL Solutions in West Virginia killed three workers.

## What this means for the plan

The base case supports a plant that blends scrap with sponge, not a 3,500 t all-scrap plant in the first three years of buying. At the plan's prices, each 1,000 t of sponge in place of scrap costs about $5M of gross margin a year.

| Year-3 case, 3,800 t of feed | Scrap   | Sponge  | Gross margin a year |
|------------------------------|---------|---------|---------------------|
| Plan, all scrap              | 3,800 t | 0       | $41M               |
| Base                         | 2,450 t | 1,350 t | $34M               |
| Base, US scrap only          | 2,050 t | 1,750 t | $32M               |
| Low                          | 700 t   | 3,100 t | $25M               |

Assumes the plan's $12/lb slab, $3.10/lb scrap delivered and cleaned, and sponge about $2.30/lb dearer than scrap. Imports average $3.49/lb, so the base case's 400 t of imports costs about $0.3M a year more than shown. Off-grade turnings cost far less raw ($0.28–0.35/lb) but lose weight in cleaning, and the cleaning-line quote will set their real cost.

1.  **Slab price.** Each $1/lb of slab price moves gross margin by $7.7M, more than the gap between the base case and all-scrap. Qualification and a named slab buyer matter more than the last 1,000 t of scrap.

2.  **Plant size.** Three fits for base-case supply. Build the 3,500 t plant and blend about 35% sponge by year 3. Or build a first hearth of about 2,000 t a year and add a second when supply proves out. Or build for 3,500 t and run it part-loaded at first, at a higher cost per pound.

3.  **Buying start.** Gate B comes before financing, about 2029, and first melt follows around 2032. To reach year-3 tonnage by Gate B, buying starts in 2027–28. Tons bought before first melt are resold to melters, so the desk runs as a small processing business. Resold within about two months, it ties up roughly $0.5–1.5M. Three ways in exist. Buy a small processor with its supplier book, sign a toll-cleaning deal with a processor such as Mega Metals, or win a Pentagon award that brings depot scrap (about 80 t in base year 3).

4.  **National security story.** Only scrap that now leaves the country or goes into steel adds American titanium. About a fifth of the base case is that kind, the 500 t through the cleaning line in year 3. Defense Industrial Base Consortium (DIBC) and Office of Strategic Capital reviewers will ask how many tons are new to US supply. The cleaning line supplies most of that answer.

5.  **Sponge risk.** A blend puts the plant in line for the same imported sponge as the incumbents, mostly from Japan. Price sponge supply in the site study, not after it.

6.  **Gate B.** The plan's Gate B needs 2,000+ t a year signed. The base case buys 2,455 t in year 3, but only the 500 t contract is plainly signed. Reaching 2,000 t signed means putting shop supply under one-year index-linked agreements. The low case doesn't reach 2,000 t within three years.

7.  **Go or no-go.** Go if the 60-day calls find at least 1,000 t a year of named, uncommitted scrap and 3+ letters of intent. Below 500 t, stop the scrap-led melt shop. The fallbacks are the plan's scrap-sourcing business or a customer-owned plant, as EcoTitanium has.

## How to prove the number in 60 days

Three data pulls and 30 generator calls test this estimate against named tons. The test is 1,000 t a year of named, uncommitted scrap (about 184,000 lb a month) and 3+ letters of intent by day 60. The model assumes about half of named tons can be won in year 1, which matches the base case's 525 t.

### Weeks 1–2: data pulls

- [ ] Register for USA Trade Online (free) and pull titanium scrap (HTS 8108.30) imports and exports by country and quantity, 2019–2026.
- [ ] Ask the USGS titanium commodity specialist for the withheld scrap figures in aggregate, and price International Titanium Association membership for its statistics.
- [ ] Ask Argus for a trial of its titanium scrap prices back to 2009.

### Weeks 2–6: calls

- [ ] 15 independent aerospace machine shops (Tier 2–3) outside the Boeing and F-35 loops
- [ ] 5 defense and space factories, such as Anduril, Hadrian, Castelion, and SpaceX or Blue Origin suppliers
- [ ] 5 implant makers in Warsaw, Indiana and Memphis
- [ ] 5 government sources, such as DLA Disposition Services, the Tinker, Ogden and Robins air logistics complexes, and a Navy Fleet Readiness Center
- [ ] 4 processors and dealers (Mega Metals, CRONIMET's United Alloys & Metals, Greystone, Goldman), for toll-cleaning prices and whether an owner would sell
- [ ] 2 equipment vendors, for a budgetary quote on a 500–1,000 t a year turnings cleaning line

Ask every generator the same five questions. Log the answers in one sheet with supplier, grade, pounds a month, current buyer, contract end date and letter-of-intent status.

1.  How many pounds a month, by grade (6-4 solids, 6-4 turnings, commercially pure)?

2.  Who buys it now, on what price formula, and when does that deal end?

3.  Does a customer contract, such as Boeing's D204, control where it goes?

4.  What would make you switch buyers?

5.  Would you sign a non-binding letter of intent?

### Weeks 6–8: decide
