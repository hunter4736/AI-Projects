# Titanium Industry Primer

2026-10-14 · @Hunter

## The industry on one page

The US melts, forges and machines titanium at scale but made no sponge, the starting metal, in 2025. It imported a record 44,000 t of sponge (about $460M) and 32,000 t of scrap instead ([USGS MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf)).

*[Figure: USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, titanium · 2025 estimates. The live doc shows the drawing.]*

- **What it is for.** Titanium matches many steels in strength at about 60% of the weight and does not corrode. Most US titanium metal goes into aerospace; most titanium ore worldwide becomes white TiO2 pigment instead, a separate business ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf)).

- **Why it costs so much.** Sponge comes from the Kroll process, a slow batch reaction. Hot titanium absorbs oxygen and nitrogen, so every melt runs in vacuum or inert gas inside water-cooled copper. Aerospace parts are machined from big plate and forgings, so most of the metal bought ends up as chips.

- **Where US sponge comes from.** Japan supplied 73% of imports from January to July 2025, Kazakhstan 13% and Saudi Arabia 13%. China and Russia, which together make about three quarters of world sponge, sell little into Western aerospace; the industry is only now weighing whether to certify Chinese sponge ([MMTA](https://mmta.co.uk/titanium-market-weighs-certification-of-chinese-sponge/)).

- **Where the US is short.** The constraint is qualified melt and feedstock, not raw furnace count. US ingot capacity was about 137,000 t a year in 2022 ([USGS Minerals Yearbook 2022](https://pubs.usgs.gov/myb/vol1/2022/myb1-2022-titanium.pdf)), yet ATI quoted about 20-month lead times for premium-quality titanium on its Q2 2026 call, against a record $4.4B backlog ([earnings call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/ati-ati-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).

- **What the rules add.** Titanium in covered defense items must be melted in the US or a qualifying country, and Japan qualifies. Imported sponge melted in Pennsylvania counts as domestic; the melt is what matters.

- **What trade policy adds.** Unwrought titanium pays a 15% duty and titanium scrap enters duty-free, so foreign scrap at about $7.70/kg roughly caps what US melters pay for comparable revert ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf)).

## From ore to part

*[Figure: titanium value chain · 6 stages, 1 revert loop. The live doc shows the drawing.]*

Every stage loses metal, so a ton of sponge ends up as a fifth of a ton or less of finished parts, and under a tenth for forged aerospace parts, and the rest is revert that can go straight back to stage 4 without new sponge. That loop is the only way to add domestic feedstock without rebuilding sponge production.

## Alloys, grades and specs

Ti-6Al-4V (Grade 5, said "six-four") is roughly half of all titanium used, so most revert you buy will be 6-4, and its value depends on keeping it apart from every other alloy ([Alloys International](https://alloysintl.com/titanium/titanium_6al-4v_eli/), a supplier estimate). Titanium people name alloys by their alloying percentages, so "6-2-4-2" is 6% aluminum, 2% tin, 4% zirconium, 2% molybdenum.

| Name you will hear                                    | Chemistry                                               | Where it goes                                                         | What it means for revert                                                                                                                             |
|-------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| CP (commercially pure) Grades 1 to 4                  | Unalloyed; oxygen and iron rise from Grade 1 to Grade 4 | Chemical plants, heat exchangers, desalination, some implants         | Sold by grade; low-oxygen CP feeds CP melts, the rest goes to ferrotitanium and aluminum alloying                                                    |
| Grade 5, Ti-6Al-4V ("6-4")                            | 6% Al, 4% V, oxygen at most 0.20%                       | Airframe structure, engine fan and compressor parts, fasteners, armor | The volume grade and the core of the business                                                                                                        |
| Grade 23, 6-4 ELI ("extra low interstitial")          | Same as 6-4, oxygen at most 0.13%                       | Implants (ASTM F136), cryogenic and fracture-critical parts           | Can go into a Grade 5 melt; Grade 5 cannot go into an ELI melt ([Harald Pihl](https://www.haraldpihl.com/en/products/titanium/titanium-grade-5/))    |
| 6-2-4-2 and 6-2-4-6                                   | Al, Sn, Zr, Mo                                          | Hot sections of engine compressors                                    | Tin, zirconium and molybdenum spoil a 6-4 heat; segregate                                                                                            |
| Beta alloys: 10-2-3, 5553, 15-3                       | Heavy V, Mo, Cr, Fe                                     | Landing gear, high-strength forgings, sheet                           | One beta lot in a 6-4 bin can ruin a furnace charge                                                                                                  |
| Grade 9, Ti-3Al-2.5V                                  | 3% Al, 2.5% V                                           | Hydraulic tubing, bike frames                                         | Segregate; close to 6-4 but not the same                                                                                                             |
| Armor alloys (for example ATI 425, Ti-4Al-2.5V-1.5Fe) | Leaner, cheaper chemistries                             | Ground vehicle and naval armor plate                                  | Phase 2 product family ([ATI 425 data sheet](https://www.atimaterials.com/Products/Documents/datasheets/titanium/alloyed/ati_425-mil_tds_en_v1.pdf)) |

Oxygen, nitrogen, carbon and hydrogen are called interstitials, and hundredths of a percent change strength and ductility. That is why heat-tinted blue or purple chips, which have picked up oxygen, sell for less than bright chips.

### Why melt practice is written into specs

In July 1989 the fan disk of United Airlines Flight 232's tail engine broke apart over Iowa. The disk had been double-VAR melted in 1971, and a hard alpha defect formed during melting went undetected ([FAA lessons learned](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/N1819U)). Engine makers responded with tighter melting, forging and inspection rules, and AMS 2380 now sets premium grades by melt practice, from double or triple VAR to a cold hearth melt followed by VAR.

A broken tungsten carbide tool tip in a chip lot becomes a high-density inclusion that VAR cannot remove, so melters pay for lots they can trust and penalize suppliers whose lots they cannot.

## Melting

Scrap-heavy titanium needs a cold hearth. VAR can only remelt a pre-built electrode and cannot remove dense inclusions; an electron beam or plasma hearth can run charges that are mostly or entirely scrap and trap tool-bit fragments before the metal solidifies. USGS lists electron beam, plasma arc, skull and vacuum arc remelting as the commercial methods for making ingot and slab ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf)).

| Method                     | How it works                                                                                                                   | Scrap tolerance                                                                               | Output                                            | Where it runs                                                                     |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| VAR (vacuum arc remelting) | An arc melts a consumable electrode into a water-cooled copper crucible under vacuum                                           | Only clean scrap pressed or welded into the electrode; dense inclusions pass straight through | Round ingot for forging                           | Every aerospace melter; the final melt for premium grades, run two or three times |
| EB cold hearth (EBCHM)     | Electron beams melt feed in hard vacuum; the metal flows across a copper hearth where dense particles sink into a frozen skull | Very high, including turnings                                                                 | Slab that rolls straight to plate, or round ingot | ATI Richland, WA; Perryman, PA; TIMET                                             |
| Plasma cold hearth (PAM)   | Plasma torches melt feed in helium or argon near atmospheric pressure, over the same kind of hearth                            | Very high, including turnings                                                                 | Slab or ingot                                     | ATI Bakers, NC; EcoTitanium, France                                               |
| Skull melting              | Induction or arc melting inside a shell of frozen titanium                                                                     | Low                                                                                           | Castings                                          | Foundries; not a mill-product route                                               |

Hard vacuum boils off some aluminum, so EB melters must compensate to hold 6-4 chemistry, while a plasma furnace's gas pressure keeps more aluminum in the metal. Either hearth followed by one VAR melt meets AMS 2380 Grades 3 and 4 for premium-quality material.

That is the price of finished ferrotitanium; scrap sold to ferrotitanium makers fetches less.

### How chips become melt-ready revert

1.  Segregate at the machine: one bin per alloy and form, no steel, no broken inserts.

2.  Weigh and log each lot at pickup: shop, alloy, part family, date.

3.  Spin out oil and coolant in a centrifuge or chip wringer.

4.  Wash in an aqueous degreaser and dry.

5.  Crush long turnings into chips and screen out fines.

6.  Pull iron with magnets; inspect solids on a belt.

7.  Analyze: handheld XRF or LIBS on solids, lab chemistry and oxygen and nitrogen on chip samples.

8.  Briquette or package, attach the lot certificate and custody record, and ship for the melter's acceptance test.

Goldman Titanium cleans, analyzes and briquettes turnings, and CRONIMET's United Alloys checks chemistry, sizing and surface against each melter's spec ([Goldman](https://www.goldmanti.com/process/turnings/), [CRONIMET USA](https://cronimetusa.com/high-temp-alloys/)).

### What lowers a lot's value

- **Mixed alloys.** One beta or 6-2-4-2 lot can push a 6-4 heat out of spec.

- **Tungsten carbide.** Broken cutting inserts become high-density inclusions.

- **Oxygen and iron.** High-oxygen or high-iron scrap goes to ferrotitanium rather than remelt ([JOM, 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11837-018-3278-1)).

- **Oil and moisture.** Both add cost to clean and carry hydrogen and carbon into the melt.

- **Contract restrictions.** Boeing's supplier clause D204 requires titanium scrap from Boeing-furnished material to go back through Boeing-designated mills, so some shop scrap is not for sale ([Boeing D204](https://www.boeingsuppliers.com/idscommon/clauses/DXXX/d204_20060501.pdf)).

## How money moves

Imported sponge cost about $460M for 44,000 t in 2025, or about $10.45/kg before the 15% duty and about $12/kg after it ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-titanium.pdf)). Each later step adds value through melting, rolling and machining, and clean turnings from the last step sell for about half the duty-paid sponge price per pound.

### Lead times and the cycle

- **Lead times.** ATI quoted about 20 months for premium-quality titanium and more than 24 months for isothermal forgings in August 2026. Mill product output runs about 12 months ahead of aircraft deliveries, so it reacts to build-rate news before the planes ship ([USGS Minerals Yearbook 2021](https://pubs.usgs.gov/myb/vol1/2021/myb1-2021-titanium.pdf)).

- **Price cycle.** Argus 6-4 bulk weldables fell from $5.00 to $5.50/lb in a 2022 sample to $2.50 to $2.90/lb in January 2026. USGS's imported scrap value fell from $8.50/kg to $7.70/kg between 2024 and 2025.

- **Price references.** The US scrap reference is Argus; Fastmarkets dropped its US titanium prices in 2023 but still publishes a quarterly US 6-4 ingot index ([MB-TI-0004](https://www.fastmarkets.com/commodity-prices/MB-TI-0004)) and European ferrotitanium.

- **Trade policy.** Titanium scrap enters duty-free and unwrought titanium pays 15%. The 2019 Section 232 investigation found sponge imports threatened national security, but in February 2020 the President chose a working group with Japan instead of tariffs ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/10/26/2021-23301/)).

## Glossary

These are the words a melter's purchasing manager or a shop owner will use in your first meetings, sorted A to Z.

| Term                      | Meaning                                                                                                                                      |
|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| AS9100                    | Aerospace quality management standard built on ISO 9001; most aerospace machine shops hold it                                                |
| Billet, bar, plate, sheet | Mill products, the forms a mill sells after forging or rolling an ingot or slab                                                              |
| Bulk weldables            | Large solid scrap pieces that can be welded directly onto a melt electrode                                                                   |
| Buy-to-fly ratio          | Weight of metal bought divided by the weight of the finished part; 10:1 means 90% became scrap                                               |
| Cold hearth melting (CHM) | Melting over a water-cooled copper hearth so dense inclusions sink and are trapped; done with electron beams (EBCHM) or plasma torches (PAM) |
| Compact                   | Sponge, master alloy and revert pressed into blocks and welded into a VAR electrode                                                          |
| DFARS specialty metals    | Defense rule that titanium in covered defense items must be melted in the US or a qualifying country                                         |
| DLA                       | Defense Logistics Agency; runs the National Defense Stockpile and sells surplus and scrap through Disposition Services                       |
| DPA Title III             | Defense Production Act authority the Pentagon uses to fund domestic production capacity                                                      |
| Electrode                 | The consumable bar that a VAR furnace melts                                                                                                  |
| ELI                       | Extra low interstitial; a low-oxygen version of an alloy, such as Grade 23                                                                   |
| Ferrotitanium (FeTi)      | Iron-titanium alloy steelmakers add to steel; the floor-price outlet for contaminated titanium scrap                                         |
| Heat                      | One melt lot; every ingot carries a heat number that traces back to its charge                                                               |
| Hard alpha (LDI)          | A brittle nitrogen- or oxygen-rich titanium defect, also called a low-density inclusion                                                      |
| HDI                       | High-density inclusion, usually tungsten carbide from broken cutting tools; VAR cannot remove it                                             |
| Ingot, slab               | The cast output of a melt; round ingots go to forging, rectangular slabs go straight to plate rolling                                        |
| Interstitials             | Oxygen, nitrogen, carbon and hydrogen; small amounts change titanium's properties                                                            |
| Kroll process             | Reduction of titanium tetrachloride with magnesium to make sponge; the standard process since the 1940s                                      |
| LECO                      | Brand name used as shorthand for gas analysis: inert gas fusion for oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen, and combustion for carbon                 |
| LIBS, XRF, OES            | Analyzers that read alloy chemistry: laser-induced breakdown, X-ray fluorescence, optical emission spectroscopy                              |
| LTA                       | Long-term agreement; the multi-year supply contracts OEMs sign with mills, which put small buyers at the back of the line                    |
| Master alloy              | Pre-alloyed additions such as aluminum-vanadium used to hit the alloy recipe                                                                 |
| MTR or cert               | Mill test report; the chemistry and property certificate that travels with each heat                                                         |
| Nadcap                    | Industry accreditation for special processes such as heat treating, chemical processing and nondestructive testing                           |
| OEM                       | Original equipment manufacturer, such as Boeing, Airbus, GE Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney                                                       |
| PQ or rotating grade      | Premium-quality titanium for rotating engine parts under AMS 2380                                                                            |
| Revert                    | Scrap that goes back into a melt; titanium people rarely say "scrap" for clean lots                                                          |
| Rutile, ilmenite          | The two titanium ores; most ore becomes white pigment, not metal                                                                             |
| Solids                    | Offcuts, plate drops and rejected parts; worth more than chips because they are easier to clean and verify                                   |
| Sponge                    | Porous titanium metal from the Kroll process; the starting metal for melting                                                                 |
| Swarf, turnings, chips    | Machining waste; curly turnings and small chips carry coolant and tool fragments                                                             |
| TiCl4 ("tickle")          | Titanium tetrachloride, the liquid intermediate made by chlorinating ore                                                                     |
| Triple VAR                | Three successive VAR melts, used for premium-quality material                                                                                |
| VAR                       | Vacuum arc remelting; melts an electrode into a water-cooled copper crucible under vacuum                                                    |
