Sources: WHOOP 5.0 / MG launch release (May 2025) · WHOOP CTO on 4.0 sensors · WHOOP, “The WHOOP approach to measuring sleep” · WHOOP Support, “WHOOP Recovery” · “How WHOOP tracks skin temperature” · FDA 510(k) K243236 (MG ECG) · Miller et al., J. Sports Sci. 2020

WHOOP is a screenless strap worn around the clock. Its main sense is photoplethysmography (PPG): LEDs shine into the skin and photodiodes measure how much light comes back. Blood absorbs light, so the signal rises and falls with every heartbeat. Green light tracks heart rate and the beat-to-beat intervals behind HRV; red and infrared light, switched on during sleep, estimate blood oxygen (SpO2). Breathing rate is derived from heart-rate rhythm. WHOOP 4.0 used 5 LEDs (3 green, 1 red, 1 infrared) and 4 photodiodes; WHOOP doesn't publish counts for 5.0 and MG, which it says capture data 26 times per second. The other senses: a 3-axis accelerometer and 3-axis gyroscope record motion; a skin-temperature sensor tracks deviation from a 90-night personal baseline; WHOOP MG adds electrode pads in the clasp for an FDA-cleared single-lead ECG that flags possible AFib. AI: machine-learning models trained against sleep-lab polysomnography classify each 30-second epoch as wake, light, REM or slow-wave sleep. Recovery combines HRV, resting heart rate, sleep performance, respiratory rate and skin temperature. Strain runs 0 to 21. Since 2023 a WHOOP Coach built on OpenAI's GPT-4 answers questions about the data. Umwelt: a sleep lab reads brain waves (EEG). WHOOP never senses the brain, so it infers sleep stages from heart and motion. Miller et al. (2020, older WHOOP hardware) found 89% agreement with polysomnography for sleep vs. wake, but 64% for four stages.
