Sources: Apple Support, “About Face ID advanced technology” · Apple Platform Security Guide, “Facial matching security” and “Biometric security” · Apple Newsroom, “The future is here: iPhone X” (2017, source of the 30,000-dot figure)

Face ID is active sensing. Like a bat that makes its own calls, the TrueDepth system makes its own light. The dot projector casts a random pattern of more than 30,000 near-infrared dots (Apple's 2017 iPhone X figure; current Apple pages say "thousands"). The flood illuminator lights the face with invisible infrared so Face ID works in the dark. The infrared camera is the actual sensor: it records the reflected dots and the flood-lit face, which become a depth map and a 2D infrared image. Those frames are digitally signed and sent to the Secure Enclave. AI: neural networks, trained on over a billion IR and depth images collected with informed consent, turn the frames into a mathematical representation and compare it with the enrolled face. Separate networks check attention (eyes open, looking at the phone) and anti-spoofing (photos, masks). Face ID also updates its stored representation over time as your appearance changes. Raw images are discarded and the face data never leaves the device. Umwelt: Face ID's world is near-infrared light and 3D shape. A printed photo has no depth, so it cannot pass as a face. Apple puts the chance that a random person unlocks the phone at under 1 in 1,000,000 with one enrolled appearance, but the odds are higher for twins, look-alike siblings and children under 13.
