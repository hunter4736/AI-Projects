Sources: CBP, Autonomous Surveillance Towers declared a program of record (2020) · Anduril, Sentry and Sentry Tower pages · DHS AI Use Case Inventory (CBP) · CBP Frontline, “Watchful Eye” · FedScoop on Sentry towers · GAO-25-107302 (2024)

Anduril's Sentry is a solar-powered surveillance tower. U.S. Customs and Border Protection runs it as an Autonomous Surveillance Tower, a program of record since 2020. Anduril says over 350 standard-range Sentry systems are operating, and CBP has ordered 200+ taller Extended Range towers. CBP describes the loop: the tower "scans the environment with radar to detect movement, orients a camera to the location of the movement detected by the radar, and analyzes the imagery using algorithms to autonomously identify items of interest, such as people or vehicles." The senses: radar works like a bat's echolocation but with radio waves. It hears echoes, so it knows where something moved but not what it is. The daylight camera adds color video. The mid-wave infrared camera senses emitted heat, a little like a pit viper's heat-sensing pits, so it works at night. AI: Lattice fuses the sensor data on the tower. DHS lists the models as machine learning trained to detect persons, vehicles and animals, with a confidence level; CBP says sorting wildlife and livestock from people means agents no longer have to watch video screens. Alerts go to agents' phones or tablets and a human makes the final call. Anduril lists about 2.8 km detection range for a person and 3.5 km for a vehicle on the standard tower. Umwelt: it senses only what is in its line of sight, and Anduril says it tracks "objects and activity, not individuals." GAO (2024) found CBP's privacy policies did not cover key protections for these towers.
